Planetary Temperature Lab

Where Earth is hottest, coldest and most unusual for the date right now. The last 24 hours of the global model analysis behind EarthView, scored point by point against the 1991–2020 normal (ERA5) and ranked by how rare the day is for that place and date, not by raw degrees, because a 4° departure in the steady tropics is stranger than a 9° one in Siberia.

Methods & limits

The live field. The hourly global grid EarthView already fetches (2,592 points at 5°), aggregated over the trailing 24 hourly frames into a per-point maximum, minimum and mean. It is model analysis at sampled grid points, not station observations: a 5° point is an area average, so the hottest point here is the hottest of the points we sample, not the hottest place on Earth. When the window includes the coarse fallback source, the page says so — that field understates extremes by several degrees.

The normal. ERA5 1991–2020, the WMO standard normal, as daily mean / max / min smoothed with four annual harmonics (two for the variance), plus percentile curves at p01 … p99 and the 1991–2020 range for every date. Each was chosen by split-half cross-validation on held-out years; the numbers are in PLANETARY_TEMPERATURE_LAB_PLAN.md §2 and the build report. The normals are 1.5° box means with no correction yet for the difference between a box and a point, and they say so.

Rarity, not degrees. "Most above normal" ranks by the empirical percentile of the 24-hour mean for that place and date. Ranked by raw degrees, two-thirds of a top ten sits poleward of 50°, because the high latitudes are simply more variable; ranked by rarity, a tropical day can lead. "Beyond 1991–2020" means beyond anything in the ERA5 reference for that date at that grid point — not a station record.

Two normals on one page. The map and the "last 24 h" reading use the 1991–2020 ERA5 normal. Your place's week and month come from Open-Meteo's forecast and are compared with the recent normal at that exact point from Open-Meteo's archive. Each says which it uses.

Data. Open-Meteo (live grid, forecast and archive) · Copernicus ERA5 via WeatherBench2 (normals) · coastlines from the NASA-derived water mask already used by EarthView.
