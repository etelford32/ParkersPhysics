7. Data, Models & Accuracy

Parkers Physics combines live observations from public scientific and government sources with our own physics-based simulations, forecasts, and hindcasts. We take the scientific integrity of these outputs seriously and make the good-faith commitments in this section.

Decision support, not a certified product. Our outputs are not certified for, and must not be the sole basis of, any safety-critical decision — including aviation, navigation, emergency management, human spaceflight, power-grid operations, satellite collision avoidance, or manoeuvre planning. Verify such decisions against authoritative sources (for example NOAA SWPC, the U.S. Space Force's Space-Track, NASA, ESA, or ICAO space-weather advisories) and your own engineering judgement.

7a. Data Provenance & Freshness

Live data comes from publicly available scientific and government sources, including without limitation:

  • NOAA SWPC — solar wind plasma and magnetic field, Kp and other geomagnetic indices, GOES X-ray flux and SUVI imagery, auroral (OVATION) forecasts, alerts, and solar region reports;
  • NASA — DONKI event catalogs (CMEs, flares, geomagnetic storms), SDO solar imagery, and the Heliophysics Event Knowledgebase;
  • JPL Solar System Dynamics — Horizons ephemerides and small-body and close-approach data;
  • CelesTrak and equivalent public catalogs — satellite orbital elements;
  • U.S. Geological Survey — ground magnetometer data;
  • National Weather Service, Open-Meteo, MET Norway, and Copernicus (CAMS) — weather, air-quality, and related terrestrial data.

Sources, cadences, and providers change over time. When a feed is delayed, unavailable, served from a fallback source, or appears anomalous in a way we can detect, we will indicate that condition on a best-effort basis (for example a "stale," "fallback," or "feed down" badge, or our status page). Archival imagery and maps are labelled with their date.

7b. Best-Effort Accuracy Commitment

We use commercially reasonable efforts to ensure that:

  • observed values are shown as received from the named source, except for unit conversion, resampling, interpolation, quality filtering, or fallback handling that is disclosed in the interface or documentation;
  • simulations and forecasts are built on published, peer-reviewed physics and standards (for example SGP4/SDP4 orbital propagation, NRLMSISE-00 thermospheric density, MHD solar-wind modelling, and flux-rope CME models) and are clearly distinguished from observations; and
  • the methods and approximations behind our models are documented well enough for an informed reader to judge their fitness for a purpose.

This is a good-faith commitment, not a warranty. It does not modify Sections 16 and 17.

7c. Model Limitations & Known Approximations

All numerical models are approximations. Known limitations include, among others:

  • Orbit propagation (SGP4/SDP4) accumulates error with time since the element set's epoch and degrades for manoeuvring, high-drag, and decaying objects. Element sets may be hours to days old.
  • Atmospheric density, drag, and decay estimates depend on empirical models and forecast space-weather drivers; errors grow sharply during geomagnetic storms, and re-entry and decay times carry wide uncertainty.
  • Solar-wind and CME models use simplified geometry, finite resolution, and idealized boundary conditions. CME arrival times typically carry uncertainties of several hours, and the magnetic orientation on arrival is hard to predict.
  • Geomagnetic, auroral, and ionospheric outputs are probabilistic and are often derived from indices or models that are not directly observed at your location.
  • Hindcasts are reconstructions of past events and do not guarantee equivalent skill on future events.
  • Visualizations may use compressed scales, exaggerated relief, or colour mappings chosen for legibility; these are disclosed where used and are not quantitative products.

7d. Alerts & Notifications

Email, browser, and in-app alerts (for example aurora, storm, or threshold alerts) are provided on a best-effort basis. They depend on upstream feeds, third-party delivery providers, and your own settings and inbox, and may be delayed, duplicated, or not delivered. Do not rely on them as your only warning of a condition that matters to you.

7e. Corrections & Errata

If we identify, or confirm a report of, a material error in a simulation, data pipeline, or display, we will in good faith (i) correct it within a reasonable time; (ii) note the correction publicly, for example in release notes or on our status page; and (iii) where the error materially affected paid users, notify affected account holders by email. Report suspected data-quality issues to accuracy@parkersphysics.com.

7f. Forecasts Are Not Promises

Forecasts, probabilities, and outlooks express our best estimate at the time they are made. Space weather and the near-Earth environment are inherently uncertain, and an event that was forecast may not occur (or may occur without having been forecast). We publish forecast verification where we can so that you can judge our skill for yourself.
