    How this is computed

    Positions. Catalogue places are J2000; your horizon is of date, so every object is precessed to today (0.36° since 2000 — about a Moon's width). The Moon is topocentric: from where you stand it sits up to 1° lower than from Earth's centre. Refraction lifts everything near the horizon by ~½°.

    Ranking. Each object's brightness, dimmed by the extra air it shines through, is compared with the faintest magnitude your sky allows right now. The difference — in magnitudes — is the ranking. Galaxies and nebulae pay a stated penalty for spreading their light over a large area, and objects near the Sun pay for glare.

    What is approximate. The visibility limit is an empirical twilight + moonlight + light-pollution model, not a radiative-transfer calculation. Nutation, aberration and proper motion (all under a few arcminutes) are left out. Planet magnitudes are the Astronomical Almanac's (Mallama & Hilton 2018).

    Data. Stars: Hipparcos (ESA 1997) via d3-celestial (BSD-3). Galaxy-map objects: this site's Galaxy Map, drawn where they lie in your sky.
