Forty-eight glaciers on every continent, measured from orbit. Every few days a Landsat or Sentinel satellite photographs each one again, and NASA's ITS_LIVE matches features between two images to measure how far the ice moved. Each new measurement is compared with that glacier's own seasonal rhythm and its four-decade record. Field mass balance, river gauges, snow stations and the weather at the glacier's own altitude sit alongside.

Where every number comes from

Motion: observed. NASA ITS_LIVE v2 (Gardner et al., The Cryosphere 2025; doi:10.5067/JQ6337239C96) measures surface velocity by feature-tracking between two images from Landsat 8/9, Sentinel-2 or Sentinel-1. A pair measures the average speed over its separation, so it is plotted at its midpoint with its own error. Only pairs 1–64 days apart are used. Each value is the median of the 3×3 pixels (120 m) around a point. The archive here was baked on —; pairs published since then are read daily by this site and are marked new.

The flowline is a streamline of the observed 2014–2024 mean field, traced from the fastest point both up- and downstream: the path ice actually takes in the data, not a hand-drawn centreline. "Upper / mid / lower glacier" are fixed fractions of it.

"Faster than usual" compares the last 45 days of pairs with ITS_LIVE's own 2014–2024 seasonal sinusoid for the same dates (v₀ + A·cos 2π(day − peak)/365.25, averaged over each pair's span). It is only claimed when the difference exceeds twice its standard error and 5 %. A spring speed-up is the glacier's normal rhythm, not news.

Mass balance: observed. World Glacier Monitoring Service glaciological series (doi:10.5904/wgms-fog), via the extract OGGM mirrors. It ends in 2018–2020 (Great Aletsch 1999); WGMS has published through 2025 and that gap is shown, not filled. Calving and ice-sheet glaciers have no field series.

Weather: reanalysis + forecast, not a measurement on the ice. ERA5 (Open-Meteo archive) and Open-Meteo forecasts, lapse-corrected to each gauge's elevation on the Copernicus GLO-30 DEM. Positive degree-days are the standard melt driver; turning them into metres of ice needs a calibrated melt model (planned), so none is claimed here.

The ice body: a model. Thickness H is a shallow-ice inversion of the observed surface speed u and the GLO-30 surface slope α (Glen's law, n = 3, A = 2.4×10⁻²⁴ Pa⁻³ s⁻¹ for temperate ice; u = 2A/(n+1)·(ρ g sin α)ⁿ·Hⁿ⁺¹ with ALL motion taken as ice deformation), capped by the perfectly-plastic bound H = τy/(ρ g sin α), τy = 150 kPa, slope smoothed over ~1 km and floored at 1.5°. Where a glacier slides the inversion over-estimates and the cap takes over; calving fronts, floating tongues and ice-sheet beds are outside what it can represent. Bed = surface − H. Driving stress = ρ g H sin α. Ice flux per unit width = 0.8·u·H. Volume and mass (ρ = 917 kg m⁻³) are for the ice inside the map window only. Vertical exaggeration and the parcels' time compression are printed on the stage.

Gauges & snow. USGS stream discharge on glacier outlet streams and NRCS SNOTEL snow-water equivalent, US glaciers only.
