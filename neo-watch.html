Earth in the middle, and everything catalogued around it — right now. Orbital elements come from JPL's Small-Body Database and are propagated in your browser to this instant; the close-approach table, the impact-risk monitor and the recent bolides are JPL's own. The globe is locked to sidereal time, so the terminator, your horizon and every fireball pin sit where they really are. For the same population on a heliocentric stage, see the solar system orrery.

Distance is drawn on a logarithmic scale; bodies are not. Near-Earth space spans four decades of radius and no linear picture holds both the ISS and 0.2 AU — but Earth and the Moon are drawn at their true relative size, so the one ratio you can check is real. The labelled rings are the ruler; True scale removes the distance compression entirely. Rings are ranges, not orbits; the Moon's path is its real one, with perigee and apogee marked. The atmosphere is a scattering model drawn to 160 km. The two amber rings are Earth's gravitational boundaries — the sphere of influence at 2.4 LD and the Hill sphere at 3.9 LD, both smaller than most people expect and both inside the lunar-distance ladder.

Positions are two-body propagation of osculating elements — exact at the element epoch and drifting from there, fastest across a close encounter. That is why the approach list beside this one is JPL's, not ours.

Method what is measured, modelled and assumed

Frames. Elements are ecliptic J2000; Earth and the Moon come from VSOP87D and Meeus ch. 47, which are of date, and are rotated back to J2000 once so everything shares one frame. Right ascension and declination are J2000; altitude and azimuth are of date and topocentric, so the diurnal parallax — 0.95° at one lunar distance, ten times that at a tenth of it — is included rather than ignored.
Sizes. A published diameter is used where JPL has one; otherwise the size is derived from absolute magnitude assuming an albedo of 0.14, and every such figure says so.
Brightness. The IAU two-parameter H, G system with G = 0.15. Past 120° of phase the fit is out of range and no magnitude is shown. Comets get none at all: their light is activity-driven.
Scale. One drawn unit is one Earth radius, and DISTANCE is logarithmic beyond the surface — nearly true out to the geostationary belt, and strongly compressed past the Moon. True scale turns it off. Body SIZES are never compressed: the drawn Moon is 0.273 of the drawn Earth, which is the real ratio.
Light. Both bodies are lit by the same Sun direction the stage derives from Earth's own ephemeris, so the terminator, the lunar phase and the sub-solar point are one calculation. Earth's atmosphere is a marched Rayleigh model — the sunset ring is the shadow test, not a painted gradient. The Moon uses the Lommel–Seeliger law rather than Lambert, which is why a full moon reads flat across the disc, as the real one does.
