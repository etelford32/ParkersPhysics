Direct-summation gravity is O(N²): 600 particles per star runs at a few tens of steps per second on a laptop; 2 500 is a coffee break. The relaxation pass before each run is what makes the stars start in equilibrium.

Over the stage: 123 views · F frame · SAB0 follow · 4 corotating · C colour · T trails · V streaks · P pathlines · X contacts · R reset · space pause. Follow moves the rig, never your orbit or zoom; any drag or wheel cancels a flight.

A neutron-star merger is the one experiment that touches every scale of physics at once: the nuclear equation of state sets the radius, the radius sets how the star deforms in its partner's tide, the tide is written into the last gravitational-wave cycles, the total mass decides whether the remnant survives, and what is thrown out decides what the telescopes see a day later. This lab runs that whole chain, and lets you change any link in it. The objects are real. The chain is the field's own: TOV structure, post-Newtonian inspiral, numerical-relativity-calibrated fits for the aftermath, and a Newtonian SPH engine with radiation reaction for the merger itself.

MEASURED · MODELLED
What is measured and what is modelled. Every object's mass, spin, field and distance is a published measurement with its source on the card; where a mass has never been measured the card says assumed. Radii marked measured are NICER's; every other radius, every tidal deformability and every fate is computed here from the equation of state you pick — eight piecewise-polytrope fits to tabulated nuclear EOSs. The merger engine is Newtonian smoothed-particle hydrodynamics with a bulk 2.5PN radiation-reaction driver — the Rasio & Shapiro / Rosswog class of simulation, not general-relativistic hydrodynamics — and its numbers are labelled SPH wherever they sit next to a calibrated fit. Nothing on this page is fetched from the network.

How it is built, and what each number is worth

Star builder

Each EOS is the piecewise-polytrope parametrisation of Read, Lackey, Owen & Friedman (2009): a four-piece SLy crust below a three-piece core described by log p₁ and three adiabatic indices. The Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff equations are integrated outward with the l = 2 metric perturbation alongside them (Hinderer 2008; Hinderer, Lackey, Lang & Read 2010), which gives the Love number k₂ and Λ = (2/3) k₂ C⁻⁵. The moment of inertia comes from the I–Love universal relation (Yagi & Yunes 2013), and a pulsar's dimensionless spin from χ = IΩ/M². The builder reproduces the published M_max to 2 %, R(1.4 M☉) to 4 % and Λ(1.4 M☉) to 25 % for all eight EOSs (node tests/star-collider-tov.mjs). A white dwarf is a Newtonian n = 1.5 polytrope.

Inspiral

TaylorT4 — the 3.5PN energy-flux balance re-expanded in x = (Mω)^{2/3} (Buonanno et al. 2009) — with the leading spin–orbit coefficient for aligned spins and the leading tidal term, whose coefficient (39/8)Λ̃ was re-derived from the tidal potential rather than copied (the derivation is in the test file). It stops at contact, evaluated with the 1PN harmonic separation, or at the test-particle ISCO, and says which. The wide, eccentric pulsar binaries in the catalogue get Peters' (1964) closed-form merger time instead, pinned to the Hulse–Taylor pulsar's 300 Myr. Spin–spin, precession and higher tidal orders are not modelled.

The merger engine

Rust compiled to WebAssembly, running in a Web Worker (rust-star-collider/, cargo test is the physics gate). Stars are Lane–Emden polytropes at the radius the TOV builder gave them, placed as a stretched lattice and relaxed with velocity damping. The hydrodynamics is cubic-spline SPH with an adaptive smoothing length, the symmetric pressure force (exact momentum conservation), Monaghan artificial viscosity, and the hybrid cold + thermal equation of state used by the first generation of merger codes (Janka et al. 1993; Bauswein et al. 2010): the polytrope is adiabatic and only shocks heat the gas. Gravity is direct summation with Plummer softening — no tree, hence the particle budget. A black hole is a point mass in a Paczyński–Wiita potential (ISCO at 6M for free) with a sink that absorbs mass and momentum. The inspiral is driven by the 2.5PN radiation-reaction acceleration of Lincoln & Will (1990) applied to the two bodies' cores as a uniform kick and integrated as E_gw; it fades once the bodies overlap. The gravitational-wave strain is the quadrupole formula evaluated from the particle accelerations, so it is exact for the Newtonian dynamics and noise-free. It is not general relativity: no GR hydrodynamics, neutrinos or magnetic fields, and the remnant's post-merger oscillation frequencies are Newtonian ones. A pinned test shows two point black holes decaying at Peters' rate to within 5 % and the 1PN orbit conserving energy to 10⁻⁸ per orbit.

Aftermath

  • Binary black holes: final spin from Barausse & Rezzolla (2009); radiated energy with the Barausse–Morozova–Rezzolla (2012) structure, its spin bracket calibrated here to the SXS equal-mass aligned-spin runs. GW150914 → 62 M☉, χ = 0.68.
  • Binary neutron stars: prompt-collapse threshold M_th = (2.38 − 3.606 C_max) M_max (Bauswein, Baumgarte & Janka 2013), with the ±0.1 M☉ scatter of that fit reported as "marginal"; hypermassive / supramassive / stable by the rigid-rotation limit 1.2 M_max. Dynamical ejecta and disk mass from Krüger & Foucart (2020); disk wind as 20 % of the disk.
  • Neutron star – black hole: remnant baryon mass from Foucart, Hinderer & Nissanke (2018), dynamical ejecta from Kawaguchi et al. (2016).
  • White dwarfs: Roche-lobe overflow (Eggleton 1983) against the companion's ISCO or surface.
  • Kilonova: the one-zone homologous model of Metzger (2017, Living Reviews) with r-process heating 2×10¹⁰ t_d^{-1.3} erg g⁻¹ s⁻¹, ε_th = 0.5, and two components differing in opacity (0.5 and 10 cm²/g). AT2017gfo-like ejecta at 40 Mpc gives ~10⁴² erg/s at one day.
  • Magnetars: vacuum-dipole spin-down, magnetic energy, and the dipole–dipole energy against the orbital binding energy at contact — the answer to "does the field matter for the orbit" is 10⁻⁶.
