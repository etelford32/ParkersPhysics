Privacy Policy

Effective Date: October 11, 2026  ·  Last Updated: October 11, 2026  ·  Version: 3.0

Telford Projects LLC, operating Parkers Physics App at parkersphysics.com ("we," "us," "our"), is committed to handling personal data lawfully, fairly, and transparently. This Policy explains what we collect, why, the legal basis, who we share it with, how long we keep it, and the rights you have. We do not sell your personal information, we do not show third-party advertising, and we do not use your data for cross-context behavioral advertising.
Most of the Service works without an account. Space-weather data, simulations and forecasts are public. Without an account we store your preferences only in your own browser.
Your location is used to answer "what does this mean where I am?" It is collected only if you share it (browser permission, a search, or a saved place), and is sent to weather / geocoding services so they can answer.
Analytics are opt-in. Google Analytics and our first-party usage analytics stay off until you accept them in the cookie banner. A Global Privacy Control signal is treated as a "no".
Minimal operational telemetry is always on. Crash reports, page-performance numbers and anonymous sign-up funnel steps, with no IP address, no email, and query strings stripped.

2. Data We Collect

We practice data minimization: we collect only what is needed to operate, secure, and improve the Service. What we process depends on how you use it.

2a. If you create an account

  • Account data — email address, display name, account ID, and either a password (stored only as a salted hash by our authentication provider) or, if you choose "Sign in with Google," the basic profile Google returns (name, email address, profile identifier). We never receive your Google password.
  • Plan & role — your subscription plan, account role, and any invite or beta code you redeemed.
  • Home location — if you set one (see Section 3).
  • Alert preferences — which notifications you turned on (for example aurora, geomagnetic storm, solar flare, CME, radio blackout, GPS/GNSS, power-grid, ionospheric disturbance, satellite pass, conjunction/collision, temperature, active-region emergence) and their thresholds, email preferences, saved places with per-place alert settings and time zone, satellites you watch, and a history of alerts sent to you.
  • Things you create — saved dashboard layouts and panel settings, Climate Lab preferences, and Spaceship Designer designs. Designs are private unless you mark one public.
  • Profile change log — a security audit record of changes to plan, role, and similar account fields (who changed what, and when).

2b. If you subscribe or pay

  • Billing records — Stripe customer, subscription, and price identifiers, subscription status, and renewal date. Payments are handled entirely by Stripe; we never receive or store your card number, and we do not store card details of any kind.

2c. If you sign up for aurora alerts (no account needed)

  • Your email address, the confirmation status of the double opt-in, any campaign (UTM) tags on the link you arrived from, and — if you provide them — your alert threshold (Kp), approximate location (latitude/longitude) and city name, so we can alert you when aurora may be visible where you are. We also record when we last alerted you, to avoid repeat emails.

2d. If you contact us

  • Request-access / enterprise inquiries — name, email, organization, role/title, use-case selections, and your message.
  • Feedback and bug reports — subject, message, optional reply-to email, and the page you were on.
  • For both forms we also store the submitting IP address and browser user-agent, used only for spam and abuse prevention.
  • Anything you send to our support, sales, or privacy email addresses.

2e. When you browse — with your consent

  • Usage analytics (only after you accept analytics in the cookie banner) — pages viewed, page titles, feature and simulation interactions, scroll depth, the referring site and campaign tags, screen size, browser language, a session identifier, and, if you are signed in, your account ID and plan. These are recorded in our own database and in Google Analytics 4 (see Section 8).
  • Session activity — when a session started and was last active, the current page, and your browser family (the user-agent with device details stripped).

2f. When you browse — always

A small amount of operational telemetry is collected from every visitor because the Service cannot be kept working without it. It contains no IP address, no email address, and no URL query strings:

  • Error reports — error messages and stack traces (with email addresses, access tokens and keys automatically redacted), the page path, and a random per-tab session ID; your account ID if you are signed in.
  • Performance measurements — page-load and responsiveness timings (Core Web Vitals) for a sample of visits.
  • Sign-in failures — the reason a sign-in failed and a shortened browser string. The email address involved is stored only as a keyed, one-way hash; plaintext emails are never written to this log.
  • Funnel & experiment steps — which step of sign-up a visitor reached, which version of a page they saw in an A/B test, and a coarse interest category inferred from the pages visited (for example "educator" or "operator"), keyed to a random visitor token stored in your browser and never joined to your identity.
  • Your cookie-banner choice — whether the banner was shown and what you chose (we have to record this before you consent; it is the consent decision itself).

Like any website, our hosting provider also receives your IP address and standard request headers in order to deliver pages (see Section 6).

What we do not collect

We do not collect biometric data, government identifiers, financial account or card numbers, health data, or other "special category" data within the meaning of GDPR Article 9. We do not read your contacts, and we do not use device fingerprinting.

3. Location Data

Much of the Service answers location-specific questions — aurora visibility, local weather and air quality, satellite passes, sky conditions, and nearby alerts. Location is always optional, and is collected only in these ways:

  • Device location — only if you press a "use my location" control and grant your browser's location permission. This is precise location; you can revoke the permission in your browser at any time.
  • Search — a place name you type into a location search.
  • Saved places / Home — locations you choose to save.

Where it goes:

  • Your current location is kept in your browser's local storage so every page can use it. If you are signed in and set a Home location or saved places, they are stored with your account so alerts can be computed for them.
  • To fetch local conditions, your browser sends the coordinates directly to the weather and geocoding services listed in Section 6 (Open-Meteo, OpenStreetMap Nominatim, and the U.S. National Weather Service). Those services receive the coordinates (or the place name you searched) and, inherently, your IP address. They are not given your name, email, or account ID.
  • Some of our own server endpoints receive coordinates in order to return localized data; we do not log them against your identity.

We do not infer your location from your IP address, we do not track your location in the background, and we do not build a location history. Precise location is "sensitive personal information" under California law; we use it only to provide the location-specific features you request.

  • Comply with legal obligations and enforce our EULA

Aurora-alert emails use your email address only for storm notifications; every message has a one-click unsubscribe link.

7. Public Data Endpoints & API Access

The data endpoints that power our pages (under /api/) serve public scientific data and do not require an account or key. If you embed or link to our pages or data from a site you operate, your visitors' browsers exchange standard request metadata (IP, user-agent, referrer) with our infrastructure; please disclose this in your own privacy notice.

12. Security

We use administrative, technical, and organizational measures appropriate to the risk, including:

  • HTTPS / TLS for all traffic to the Service
  • Encryption at rest for the production database
  • Passwords stored only as salted bcrypt hashes by our authentication provider; we never see them
  • Row-Level Security in our database so each account can read only its own data
  • Keyed one-way hashing of email addresses in sign-in-failure logs, and automatic redaction of emails, tokens, and keys from error reports
  • An audit log of changes to account roles and plans
  • Least-privilege access for staff and server functions, rate limiting, and spam traps on public forms
  • Monitoring, logging, and dependency updates

No system is perfectly secure. If you suspect unauthorized access to your account or have found a vulnerability, contact security@parkersphysics.com. Where required by law, we will notify affected users and supervisory authorities of personal-data breaches without undue delay (and within 72 hours under GDPR Article 33 where feasible).

14. Your Rights (GDPR & UK GDPR)

If you are in the EEA, the United Kingdom, or Switzerland, you have the following rights. Exercising them is free, and we will respond within one month (extendable by two further months for complex requests, with notice).

  • Access (Art. 15) — confirmation of whether we process your data, and a copy of it.
  • Rectification (Art. 16) — correct inaccurate or incomplete data. Most account fields can be edited directly in your account settings.
  • Erasure (Art. 17) — request deletion in defined circumstances.
  • Restriction (Art. 18) of processing.
  • Portability (Art. 20) — receive your data in a structured, machine-readable format.
  • Objection (Art. 21) — object to processing based on our legitimate interests.
  • Withdraw consent (Art. 7(3)) — at any time, for example through Cookie Settings, your browser's location permission, or the unsubscribe link in any alert email.
  • Not to be subject to solely automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects (Art. 22) — see Section 17.
  • Complain to your supervisory authority (e.g., the Irish DPC, the UK ICO, the Swiss FDPIC) or the authority where you live, work, or where the alleged infringement occurred.

To exercise any right, email privacy@parkersphysics.com from the address on your account. We may need to verify your identity before responding.

15. California Privacy Rights (CCPA / CPRA)

If you are a California resident, you have the following rights:

  • Right to correct inaccurate personal information.
  • Right to opt out of sale or sharing. We do not sell or share personal information for cross-context behavioral advertising. We honor Global Privacy Control signals as an opt-out preference.
  • Right to limit use of sensitive personal information. The sensitive personal information we process is account log-in credentials and, if you provide it, precise geolocation. We use them only to authenticate you and to provide the location-specific features you request — uses that do not give rise to the right to limit.
  • Right to non-discrimination for exercising any of these rights.

Categories collected in the prior 12 months: identifiers (A); customer records (B); commercial information (D); internet or other electronic network activity (F); geolocation, including precise geolocation if you provide it (G); professional information you submit in an access request (I); and inferences — a coarse interest category used to tailor page content (K). Sources: you, your browser, Google (if you use Google Sign-In), and Stripe. Categories disclosed for a business purpose: A, B, D, F, G, I, and K to the service providers in Section 6. Categories sold or shared: none.

To exercise these rights, email privacy@parkersphysics.com, or have an authorized agent submit a request with documentation of authority.

"Shine the Light" (Cal. Civ. Code §1798.83): we do not share personal information with third parties for their direct marketing purposes.

Automated rate limiting, spam filtering, and plan-based feature access are reviewed by a person if you challenge an outcome.
