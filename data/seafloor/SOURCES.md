# data/seafloor — sources and what is measured

Baked for `sea-floor.html`. Never hand-edit the files here; re-run the bakes.

| File | What | Baked by |
|------|------|----------|
| `index.json` | grid geometry + the deepest cell FOUND in each window | `scripts/build-seafloor-bathymetry.py` |
| `<id>.bin.gz` | one 1 arc-minute bathymetry window per site (int16 LE metres of elevation, row 0 north) | same |
| `world.bin.gz` | the whole planet at 0.25° for the globe (10 m steps, row-delta coded, byte-shuffled) | same |
| `casts.json.gz` | the 8 deepest real temperature casts within 150 km of each site | `scripts/build-seafloor-casts.py` |

## Bathymetry — a grid, not a survey

Source: the public **AWS Terrain Tiles** bucket (`s3.amazonaws.com/elevation-tiles-prod`, Terrarium
encoding; Mapzen/Tilezen). Its ocean is **NOAA ETOPO1** (1 arc-minute, doi:10.7289/V5C8276M) with
**GEBCO 2014** where finer. Most deep-ocean cells are depth PREDICTED from satellite altimetry,
tied to sparse ship tracks. Expect tens to a few hundred metres of difference from a multibeam or
lander sounding in a trench; `js/seafloor/bathy.js` `GRID_VERTICAL_UNCERTAINTY_M` (150 m) is a
stated order of magnitude, not a measured error of this bake.

gebco.net and ncei.noaa.gov were egress-blocked at build time; the AWS bucket is the one
bathymetry host that was reachable. A direct GEBCO 2024 (15 arc-second) bake would be the upgrade.

Attribution: ETOPO1 — NOAA NCEI (public domain). GEBCO — GEBCO Compilation Group. Terrain Tiles —
Mapzen / Linux Foundation, see https://github.com/tilezen/joerd/blob/master/docs/attribution.md

## Diving anywhere — read live, same source

Away from the 28 baked windows, `sea-floor.html` reads the SAME tiles at the moment of the dive,
through the existing `/api/climalytix/dem` route (no new route, no passthrough URL), and samples
them onto the same 1′ grid (`js/seafloor/free-dive.js`). A missing tile is a failed dive, never a
flat sea floor. Tiles stop at 85° of latitude; dives stop at 80°.

## Temperature casts — measured

Source: **NOAA NCEI World Ocean Database** (Boyer et al.), the public `noaa-wod-pds` S3 bucket:
`ctd` and `osd` (bottle) files for every year, `pfl` (profiling floats) from 2014. US Government
work, public domain; cite the World Ocean Database.

Kept as archived: in-situ temperature (°C) and practical salinity. Levels flagged by WOD's own
quality control are DROPPED, never repaired. Profiles are thinned (never averaged). No
IPTS-68 → ITS-90 conversion (millikelvin at these temperatures).

The baker reads byte RANGES of the per-year netCDF files (every variable is contiguous and
uncompressed), so a year costs about a megabyte of transfer rather than the 0.1–3.5 GB file.

## What is MODEL on the page

- Sunlight with depth: Beer–Lambert with K_d = 0.04 m⁻¹ (`js/seafloor/light.js`), written from
  memory and marked unverified. The dive view's clear water and lit sea floor are display choices.

- Temperature below a cast's deepest level: the adiabat (`js/seafloor/profile.js`), labelled.
- Sound speed, potential temperature, pressure↔depth: UNESCO 1983 (EOS-80) algorithms in
  `js/seafloor/seawater.js`, gated against `tests/fixtures/seafloor/seawater-oracle.json`
  (generated by python-seawater and gsw).
- "Commonly quoted" depths in `js/seafloor/sites.js`: written from memory, `verified: false`.

## Plates and slabs — imported, not baked here

The page reads the Inner Earth page's own files, through its own kernels:

- `data/earth-interior/plates-pb2002.json.gz` — Peter Bird's PB2002 plate model (boundaries,
  Euler poles, boundary-step table). Credit Bird (2003) and Ahlenius/Nordpil (ODC-By).
- `data/earth-interior/slab2.json.gz` — USGS Slab2 (Hayes et al. 2018), top of the subducted plate.

A closing or sliding rate is the rigid rotation of one plate relative to the other at that spot.

- The bending plate (`js/seafloor/trench-model.js`): the universal elastic-plate trench profile
  (Caldwell et al. 1976; Turcotte & Schubert) fitted to the median of nine parallel sections of
  this page's own bathymetry. MODEL. The effective elastic thickness is printed only when the fit
  passes its tests; E, ν and the densities are textbook constants. The formula was written from
  the textbook derivation and is checked by its own identities and by planted-plate recovery, not
  against published thicknesses for these trenches (no literature access at build time).
