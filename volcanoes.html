    How it works

    Catalogue (observed). The Smithsonian Global Volcanism Program's Volcanoes of the World 5.3.4 lists, downloaded 22 March 2026: 1,222 Holocene volcanoes and 1,439 Pleistocene volcanoes, plus GVP's eruption table (start year, VEI) and population counts as exported in 2020. Baked by scripts/build-volcano-catalog.mjs with every source file's hash pinned.

    Status (a rule on the record). Erupting: last eruption 2025–26 · Recently active: since 1900 · Historical: dated CE before 1900 · Dormant: Holocene, dated BCE or undated but confirmed · Unconfirmed: GVP rates the Holocene evidence uncertain · Inactive: no known Holocene eruption. "Inactive" is not "extinct".

    Eruption odds (model). Onsets since 1900 per volcano, shrunk toward the whole Holocene population with an empirical-Bayes Gamma–Poisson prior fitted by maximum likelihood; the chance of a new onset in Δt is the closed-form predictive. A decades-long eruption counts once, so persistently active vents (Stromboli, Santa María) read lower than how often they are in eruption.

    Climate chain (model). VEI → erupted volume (Newhall & Self 1982) → column height (Mastin et al. 2009, with a stated climactic duration) → plume top vs a latitude-dependent tropopause → SO₂ (a log-linear fit to named eruptions; real sulfur yields scatter by ~3×) → global aerosol optical depth (Pinatubo-anchored) → forcing (−21 W m⁻² per unit AOD) → a one-box energy balance (C = 8 W yr m⁻² K⁻¹, λ = 1.2 W m⁻² K⁻¹). Pinatubo 1991 through this chain gives −3.1 W m⁻² and −0.34 K (observed ≈ −3 W m⁻², ≈ −0.4 K). It is a consequence calculator, not a climate model.
