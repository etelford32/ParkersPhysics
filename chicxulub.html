Chicxulub, backwards

Sixty-six million years ago something hit the Yucatán shelf and ended the Cretaceous. Nobody saw it. What exists is a buried crater, a thin layer of iridium around the whole planet, and beds of debris that thin with distance. This page starts from those measurements and works backwards — to the size and speed of the body, the direction it came from and the orbit it was on — and then forwards again to what it did.

Tiles show the reconstruction's median and 68 % range for a main-belt asteroid. It is a scaling-law reconstruction, not a shock-physics simulation; the scaling coefficients and some evidence values were written from memory of the literature and are marked as unchecked below.

    What this is, and what it is not

    Crater scaling. Impactor → transient crater → final rim uses the Collins, Melosh & Marcus (2005) scaling laws, with no shock physics, no layered target and no sea. The laws predict the topographic rim, taken here as the 140–170 km inner rim; reading the 180–200 km outer ring as the rim instead makes the body about a fifth larger and nearly doubles the energy. That choice is the largest uncertainty on this page.

    Iridium. The layer's 55 ng/cm² over the whole Earth is about 280,000 tonnes of iridium. In a CM-type body that is the iridium of a ~7 km asteroid. A 7 km body would need to arrive faster than anything bound to the Sun can to dig this crater, and the ruthenium isotopes say it was an asteroid, not a comet. So the reconstruction lets the layer hold only a fraction of the impactor's iridium and reports that fraction. Where the rest went — the crater, the nearby ejecta, or off the planet — is not modelled.

    Trajectory. Exact two-body mechanics from the ground out of Earth's gravity, then Earth's orbital velocity added. It uses today's site latitude, today's axial tilt and a circular orbit for Earth; none of the three is known for 66 million years ago. "From the north-east" is read as a bearing of 45° ± 30°.

    Climate. One box with one heat capacity. The length of the darkness is taken from the published climate-model runs, not predicted; the box predicts only the temperature, and with a shallow ocean layer it over-cools about fourfold. It is here to show what the published answer implies, not to replace it.
